\section{Limitations}
\begin{frame}{Limitations}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{No unified evaluation framework}

        
        Existing studies depend on precision and recall alone, failing to capture ranking stability, reasoning consistency, or agent autonomy. Incompatible protocols across papers make meaningful cross-study comparison impossible.

        \item \textbf{Fragmented, siloed optimization}


        Retrieval, fusion, and generation modules are tuned independently with no unified pipeline view. Improvements in one stage rarely produce proportional gains in overall system output.

        \item \textbf{Retrieval instability is largely ignored}


        Static and single-pass systems produce inconsistent rankings across repeated or slightly varied queries. This ranking noise propagates directly into generation, producing contradictory or hallucinated responses.


        \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations...}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{High overhead with no middle-ground solution}


        Multi-Encoder and Knowledge Graph methods incur excessive storage and compute costs, while \textbf{RRF} alone offers insufficient semantic depth. No existing system bridges this gap for scalable, high-quality retrieval.

        \item \textbf{Narrow validation, domain-locked, and not generalisable}


        Most architectures are tested on small, curated datasets with weak baselines and optimised for a single domain such as healthcare or finance. They lack the flexibility to transfer across domains or scale beyond controlled lab conditions - revealing a critical gap in real-world applicability.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
